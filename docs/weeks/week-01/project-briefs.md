# Directors Notes Prototype Briefs

## The Client Context

[Directors Notes](https://directorsnotes.com/) is an independent UK film
publication. It publishes short films alongside interviews that explore the
decisions, craft and people behind them. Its archive contains almost twenty
years of films and filmmaking stories.

Directors Notes is redesigning how visitors explore that archive. MarBelle,
Co-Founder and Editor-in-Chief, will introduce the opportunity at the start of
the bootcamp and return for the Week 8 prototype presentations.

## Your Project

Choose one brief from this document and develop an individual prototype across
the eight-week bootcamp.

Your project must remain closely connected to its Directors Notes brief. You
will make your own decisions about its visual design, content hierarchy,
interactions, accessibility and implementation.

Several learners may choose the same brief. Different responses to the same
problem are valuable and expected. Every learner creates their own repository,
project board, code and presentation.

## What You Are Making

You are making an independent learning prototype, not production software for
the live Directors Notes website. Your work does not need to be complete or
polished to be valuable.

In Week 8 you will present the prototype to MarBelle. Your ideas, interactions
or visual approach may inspire future Directors Notes development, but there is
no expectation that your work will be adopted or published.

## Asset Boundary

Until Directors Notes grants explicit permission, do not copy or download its:

- Photographs or film stills
- Videos
- Logos
- Article or interview text
- Other copyrighted visual assets

Use fictional film information, your own words and artwork, openly licensed or
clearly attributed assets, abstract placeholders, or designs created with HTML
and CSS. Record the source and licence for any third-party material you use.

This boundary is an opportunity to invent a visual language rather than copy
the existing website.

## How To Read the Briefs

Every brief has the same structure:

- **Opportunity** explains why the project could be useful.
- **Intended visitor** identifies who you are designing for.
- **Starting Point** is the smallest meaningful prototype.
- **Possible Directions** are optional ideas, not a required feature list.
- **Keep Manageable** protects the project from becoming too large.

Everyone begins with the Starting Point. Add another direction only when the
existing prototype works and you can explain the code.

# Brief 1: Film Discovery Explorer

## Opportunity

A large archive becomes more useful when visitors can narrow it according to
what they want to watch. Explore a simple way to browse a small collection of
films using meaningful characteristics.

## Intended Visitor

Someone who wants to find a short film but does not already know its title or
filmmaker.

## Starting Point

- Display a small collection of fictional films as cards.
- Give every film a title and one shared characteristic, such as form, subject
  or country.
- Let the visitor filter the collection using that one characteristic.

## Possible Directions

- Add a second filter.
- Include runtime or year.
- Show the number of matching films.
- Provide a helpful message when nothing matches.
- Remember the visitor's most recent filter.

## Keep Manageable

Use a small local collection of films. Do not connect to the live Directors
Notes archive or attempt to build a production search engine.

# Brief 2: Filmmaker Profile

## Opportunity

A filmmaker's work may appear across several interviews. Explore how one page
could introduce that person and make their body of work easier to understand.

## Intended Visitor

Someone who has discovered one filmmaker and wants to see what else they have
made.

## Starting Point

- Introduce one fictional filmmaker.
- Display a short biography in your own words.
- Present three films as a small filmography.
- Give each film a clear title, year and form.

## Possible Directions

- Filter the filmography by form or year.
- Highlight a recommended starting film.
- Reveal further details when a film is selected.
- Add fictional collaborators or festival appearances.
- Let the visitor bookmark one film.

## Keep Manageable

Build one profile with a small filmography. Do not create a database of many
filmmakers or reproduce a real filmmaker's biography without permission.

# Brief 3: Film Folio

## Opportunity

Important information about a film can be difficult to find when it is buried
inside a long interview. Explore a clear, readable way to present a film's
essential facts alongside its story.

## Intended Visitor

Someone reading about a film who wants to understand its key details, credits
and achievements.

## Starting Point

- Present one fictional film.
- Include a title, short description, runtime, form and country.
- Include a short credit list.
- Organise the information into a clear visual hierarchy.

## Possible Directions

- Add festival appearances or an award result.
- Let the visitor expand and collapse longer sections.
- Add a second film to test whether the design is reusable.
- Provide a copyable credits view.
- Remember whether the visitor prefers a compact or expanded display.

## Keep Manageable

Concentrate on presenting information clearly. Do not recreate an entire
interview page, video player or content-management system.

# Brief 4: Awards Journey

## Opportunity

Festival and award language can be difficult to interpret. Explore how to make
a film's progress from selection to winning understandable at a glance.

## Intended Visitor

Someone who wants to understand what recognition a film received and what the
different results mean.

## Starting Point

- Present one fictional film and one award or festival journey.
- Show a sequence such as Official Selection, Longlisted, Shortlisted,
  Nominated and Winner.
- Clearly indicate the highest result reached by the film.

## Possible Directions

- Explain each result in plain language.
- Compare the journeys of two or three films.
- Filter fictional films by their highest result.
- Add an accessible text alternative to the visual journey.
- Let a visitor save a film for later.

## Keep Manageable

Use a small, invented dataset and one consistent result sequence. Do not model
every real festival, award body or exception.

# Brief 5: Festival Explorer

## Opportunity

Festivals connect films, filmmakers, places and moments in time. Explore a
small destination that helps a visitor understand one festival programme or
year.

## Intended Visitor

Someone interested in discovering films connected to a particular festival.

## Starting Point

- Introduce one fictional festival or festival edition.
- Display a small selection of associated films.
- Include a useful characteristic such as form, country or result.
- Let the visitor select a film to learn more.

## Possible Directions

- Filter by form or result.
- Compare two festival years.
- Highlight a fictional winner or staff recommendation.
- Add a simple schedule or location view.
- Remember the visitor's selected festival year.

## Keep Manageable

Begin with one festival and a small number of films. Do not build ticketing,
maps, accounts or a complete festival-management system.

# Brief 6: What Should I Watch?

## Opportunity

Visitors do not always want to search a large archive. Explore a friendly route
that recommends one film from a few simple choices.

## Intended Visitor

Someone willing to watch a short film but unsure where to begin.

## Starting Point

- Ask the visitor two or three simple questions.
- Use the answers to recommend one fictional film.
- Explain why the film was recommended.
- Let the visitor start again.

## Possible Directions

- Use runtime, form, subject or viewing mood as choices.
- Offer an alternative recommendation.
- Show how changing one answer affects the result.
- Remember the visitor's most recent choices.
- Make the interaction keyboard-accessible and screen-reader friendly.

## Keep Manageable

Use simple rules and a small local film collection. Do not build an account,
machine-learning system or connection to a streaming service.

# Brief 7: Thematic Archive Story

## Opportunity

An archive can be explored as an editorial story rather than only through
search results. Explore a guided journey through films connected by one theme.

## Intended Visitor

Someone who wants an editor to help them discover an interesting group of
films.

## Starting Point

- Choose one theme and describe it in your own words.
- Present three fictional films connected to that theme.
- Explain briefly why each film belongs in the story.
- Give the journey a clear beginning, middle and end.

## Possible Directions

- Add a visual progress indicator.
- Let the visitor choose between two paths through the story.
- Reveal films one at a time.
- Add a related subject at the end.
- Remember where the visitor reached.

## Keep Manageable

Create one short editorial journey. Do not attempt to classify the whole
Directors Notes archive or write full-length film criticism.

# Brief 8: Homepage Discovery Rail

## Opportunity

A homepage can surface useful parts of an archive instead of showing only the
newest material. Explore one focused section that could help visitors discover
something worth watching.

## Intended Visitor

Someone arriving at Directors Notes without a specific film in mind.

## Starting Point

- Choose one clear rule for the section, such as Under Ten Minutes, From the
  Archive or Award Recognised.
- Display a small row or grid of fictional films.
- Explain the section clearly enough that a visitor understands why the films
  appear together.

## Possible Directions

- Let the visitor move through more films.
- Add an editorially featured item.
- Offer a second view or sorting choice.
- Show a useful piece of metadata on every card.
- Remember a display preference.

## Keep Manageable

Prototype one homepage section, not the entire homepage. Use a fixed local
dataset rather than attempting to automate editorial publishing.

# Brief 9: Submission Readiness Guide

## Opportunity

Filmmakers need to understand whether their work is suitable before submitting
it to a publication. Explore a supportive guide that makes requirements and
next steps easier to understand.

## Intended Visitor

A filmmaker considering submitting a film to Directors Notes.

## Starting Point

- Present a short set of fictional or tutor-supplied submission requirements.
- Let the visitor respond to each requirement.
- Provide a clear summary of what they should check next.
- Make clear that the prototype is not an official eligibility decision.

## Possible Directions

- Add explanations for unfamiliar requirements.
- Show progress through the guide.
- Provide different next steps based on the answers.
- Let the visitor reset the guide.
- Remember incomplete progress on the same device.

## Keep Manageable

This is an explanatory prototype, not a real submission form. Do not collect
personal information, upload films, take payments or invent official Directors
Notes policy.

# Brief 10: Related Film Journey

## Opportunity

Every film can become a route into another part of the archive. Explore how one
film could lead naturally to another through a shared subject, person, form,
place, festival or award.

## Intended Visitor

Someone who has finished reading about or watching one film and wants a
meaningful next choice.

## Starting Point

- Begin with one fictional film.
- Offer two or three related fictional films.
- State the connection behind each recommendation.
- Let the visitor choose one next film.

## Possible Directions

- Let the journey continue through several films.
- Offer different relationship types.
- Show a simple visual map of the connections.
- Prevent the visitor immediately returning to the same film.
- Remember films already visited during the journey.

## Keep Manageable

Use a small hand-written network of films and relationships. Do not build a
general recommendation algorithm or reproduce the complete Directors Notes
archive.
